# ADR-0016: External identity providers: OIDC sign-in per organization
Status: Accepted (the recommended decisions, taken on your instruction to make them; amend or reverse them here) · Roadmap: SSO-1 · Amends: ADR-0006

## Context
ADR-0006 says humans sign in with GitLab OAuth, and that SSO beyond GitLab is backlog. An organization that runs its people through Okta, Entra, Google, Auth0 or Keycloak wants them to sign in there. Today `users.gitlab_id` is required and unique, and every role comes from the organization's linked GitLab group (AUTH-5). This ADR changes the auth model, so it is a human gate; the choices below are the recommended ones.

## Decision
1. **OIDC only.** Authorization code with PKCE, `state` and `nonce`, the `id_token` checked against the issuer's JWKS with the verifier CI already uses (`id-token.ts`: RS256 and RSA keys of 2048 bits or more, `iss`, `aud`, `exp`, `nbf`). **No SAML**: it needs an XML signature parser nobody should write by hand, and a dependency. SSO-8 is deleted. A provider that signs its `id_token` only with ES256 or EdDSA cannot be used in v1.
2. **One provider per organization, and GitLab sign-in stays for everyone.** An organization cannot *require* SSO in v1: SSO adds a second way in, it does not close the first. Removing someone completely means removing them from the IdP's groups and from the organization's GitLab group.
3. **Identity model.** A person is a `users` row with one or more rows in `user_identities(provider, subject)`, unique as a pair. `provider` is `gitlab` or `sso:<provider id>`, so two issuers that both call someone `1` are two identities. The key is the issuer's `sub`, never an email, a name or a username. `users.gitlab_id` becomes nullable (a person who only signs in through an IdP has none) and stays unique; existing users get a `gitlab` identity by backfill.
4. **Provider settings** (`sso_providers`, one per organization): issuer, client id, the client secret sealed like a connection's (own data key, wrapped by the master key, ADR-0007, so it is in the re-wrap list and write-only in the API), the allowed email domains, the role map, and `enabled` (off when made). Turning it on needs at least one domain and one role rule. A disabled or deleted provider signs nobody in **and** its memberships stop counting at once.
5. **Roles: a map, deny by default.** A rule is `claim` + `value` + `role`: it matches when the `id_token`'s claim (top level only) equals the value, or is a list that contains it. The highest role of the matching rules is granted; no match means the sign-in is refused and nothing is stored. The map can only name roles of the provider's own organization (the membership has no organization column of its own: it is the provider's), so one organization's IdP cannot grant a role in another.
6. **Also required at every sign-in:** `email` present with `email_verified` exactly `true`, and the email's domain (compared whole, case-insensitively, not as a suffix) in the provider's list. A multi-tenant IdP such as Google would otherwise accept anyone.
7. **Membership is re-evaluated at each sign-in and expires.** A sign-in replaces the person's membership for that provider and sets it to end when the session would (the session length: 12 hours). Roles are read from it on every request, only while it is live and its provider is enabled. So a group removed at the IdP costs the person their access by the next sign-in, and at the latest after one session length; a linked person whose GitLab roles go on keeps those, not the SSO ones. The cost: an API token owned by an SSO person carries no SSO roles after that, so scripts need the person to sign in again, or to use CI's own `id_token`.
8. **Linking is explicit.** The first sign-in from an IdP identity that no person holds creates a **new** person, whatever email it asserts: never a link by email. An identity is linked to an existing person only by that person, from a session they already hold, by completing the provider's sign-in. The spec's second path (a "trusted" provider linking on a verified email) is **not built**: the platform stores no verified email for a GitLab person to compare with, so such a link would trust an IdP's claim about someone else's account. An identity held by one person cannot be linked to another. **Starting a link is a POST** (`POST /auth/sso/:slug/link`), never a GET: the session cookie is `SameSite=Lax`, so another site could make a signed-in browser send a GET, and a link to a provider its owner controls would give that owner a sign-in as the person (found by the feature's security review). Even so, a person who links a hostile organization's provider on purpose hands it their account: the page says so, and the real remedy (a session started by a provider carrying only that provider's roles, and no token creation from it) would reverse decision 7's merge of roles, so it is an open decision, not built. A person can unlink an identity of their own unless it is their last; unlinking an SSO identity ends that provider's membership.
9. **Logout** ends the platform session, as for GitLab. No RP-initiated logout at the IdP (the IdP's own session is its business).
10. **No platform-admin role from an IdP.** `ORCH_PLATFORM_ADMIN_GITLAB_IDS` stays the only way to be one, so a person without a GitLab identity is never a platform admin.
11. **Who may set this up.** Reading a provider's settings (never its secret) is an org admin's, creating, changing, testing, enabling and deleting it is an org **owner's**: whoever controls the role map decides who signs in as what, and an admin must not be able to map their way to owner.
12. **The IdP is an outbound target.** Its address is typed by an org owner, so every fetch (discovery, token, keys) uses the same public-address rule as notification sinks (https, no private, loopback or metadata addresses, the resolved address checked too). Discovery's `issuer` must equal the configured one exactly, and the endpoints it names must pass the same rule.
13. **Tests use a fake IdP shaped like Keycloak** (discovery document, JWKS, a token endpoint that spends a code once). Nothing in the tests contacts a real IdP; **the first real sign-in is a human gate**.

## Consequences
- ADR-0006 stands for scripts (hashed scoped tokens) and CI (`id_token`); humans may now also sign in through their organization's IdP.
- `users.gitlab_id` is nullable: code that read it must handle a person with none. A person with none holds roles only from the IdP's map.
- A removed group takes effect at the next sign-in or one session length later, not instantly. SCIM and back-channel logout are out of scope.
- GitLab connections, and GitLab sign-in for the people who need them, are unchanged.
- Not covered: SAML, enforcing MFA (the IdP's job), organization custom domains, an org requiring SSO, SSO for the CLI other than through the browser sign-in it already uses, and any check that the IdP is who it says.
- Reversing this: drop `sso_providers`, `sso_memberships` and `user_identities` and make `users.gitlab_id` required again once no person is without one. The migration is additive.
